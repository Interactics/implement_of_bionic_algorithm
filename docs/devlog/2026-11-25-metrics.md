# 2026-11-25 · Evaluation metrics (paper Tab. 3)

**Blocks changed:** B8 Evaluation (new: `scripts/metrics.py`) · see [architecture](../architecture.md)

## What changed
- New `scripts/metrics.py`. It scores a humanoid `qpos` sequence with the four kinematic metrics from ReActor's Tab. 3, and has a self-check (`--check`).
- Ran it on all five IK retargeting results (`outputs/retarget/*.npz`).

## Why
- The course task asks to "evaluate motion fidelity, foot sliding and collisions". Until now we only judged GIFs by eye.
- The bilevel ReActor loop (planned) needs these numbers as its objective.
- No training needed, so it could run while the night queue uses the CPU.

## How it works
For every frame: set `qpos`, run `mj_forward`, read MuJoCo's contacts and distances.

| Metric | Definition (paper Tab. 3) | Our implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Ground penetration | fraction of frames with penetration > 1 cm; depth = mean over those frames (deepest contact per frame) | contacts with the floor geom, depth $=-\text{dist}$ |
| Self-penetration | same, body vs body; ignore pairs on the same kinematic chain | contacts between non-floor geoms whose bodies are not ancestor/descendant |
| Foot sliding | mean linear foot velocity during *reference* contact | horizontal speed of the foot capsules' centre, in frames where the source foot is in contact |
| Foot floating | mean min foot–ground distance during reference contact | `mj_geomDistance(foot capsule, floor)`, min over the 2 capsules, clipped at 0 |

**Reference contact** (which frames the human foot is planted), simplified from Shimada et al. 2020: the source ankle or toe joint is in contact when it is

$$z - z_{5\%} < 5\,\text{cm} \quad\text{and}\quad \|\dot{\mathbf p}_{xy}\| < 0.3\,\text{m/s}.$$

So sliding is "how fast the robot foot moves while the human foot stands still".

## How verified
- Self-check: a standing pose in the air gives 0 penetration and 0 sliding. The same pose sunk into the floor gives 100 % penetration time and > 1 cm depth. Moving it 1 cm per frame at 30 fps gives 30 cm/s sliding.
- The self-penetration pairs make sense when inspected. On the walks they are mostly lower arm or hand against pelvis or thigh: the robot's capsules are wider than a human's hips, so a natural arm swing clips them.

- **Reading:** IK shows every artifact the paper lists.
  - Feet slide at 20–45 cm/s while the human's feet are planted.
  - The feet penetrate the floor in 12–59 % of frames, because a single height offset per clip can't fix every frame.
  - Arms clip through the hips on the walks.
  - For scale: in the paper's worst-case table (Lima robot), the baselines' foot sliding reaches about 95–99 cm/s.
- Caveats:
  - The contact thresholds are our own choice.
  - The approximate SMPL skeleton (ADR 0002) shifts foot heights by a few cm.
  - The paper's absolute numbers come from other robots (G1, Lima), so compare trends, not values.

## Deviations from the paper
- Contact estimation uses a height + velocity threshold, not Shimada's full method. Same idea, simpler.
- Foot position is the centre of the foot capsules, not a contact point.
