# Quiz 04 · Evaluation metrics (2026-11-25)

Covers: [metrics devlog](../devlog/2026-11-25-metrics.md).

---

**Q1. Why measure foot sliding only during *reference* contact frames?**

> [!success]- Answer
> A swinging foot is supposed to move fast. Sliding is only an artifact when the human's foot is planted and the robot's foot still moves. So the source motion decides when a foot "should" be still.

**Q2. Why do we need the human's contact labels at all? Couldn't we just use the robot's own floor contacts?**

> [!success]- Answer
> If the robot's foot floats above the floor, it has no contact, so it would never be counted as sliding or floating. The artifact would hide itself. The reference tells us what *should* happen, independent of the robot's mistakes.

**Q3. IK puts the feet into the floor in 12–59 % of frames even though `ground()` shifts the clip so the feet stand on the floor. Why?**

> [!success]- Answer
> `ground()` applies one vertical offset per clip, at the 5th percentile of the lowest foot. Frame by frame the foot height still varies, because the bone lengths and joint limits differ from the human's. Frames lower than that percentile sink in. Fixing it per frame would cause floating instead, which is the trade-off the paper describes for OmniRetarget.

**Q4. Why ignore self-contacts between bodies on the same kinematic chain?**

> [!success]- Answer
> Neighbouring capsules (thigh and shin at the knee, for example) overlap by construction at the joints. Counting them would report penetration in every frame even for a perfect motion. The paper does the same to remove these false positives.

**Q5. On the walks, the self-penetration is mostly hand or lower arm against thigh or pelvis. Why does a normal human arm swing collide on the robot?**

> [!success]- Answer
> The IK copies the human's arm *direction*, but the robot's hip and thigh capsules are wider than a human's. Retargeting only keypoints knows nothing about body volume. A physics-based method sees the collision in simulation and moves the arm out.

**Q6. What should change in this table after RL tracking, and what might not?**

> [!success]- Answer
> - **Should drop:** ground penetration, because the simulator's floor contact pushes back, and self-penetration, because the capsules collide.
> - **May not reach zero:** foot sliding and floating. The policy doesn't strictly enforce the reference contact pattern, which the paper itself notes for ReActor.
> - **Tracking error will rise a little:** that's the price of physical feasibility.
