# Quiz 03 · Stabilized training (2026-11-25)

Covers: [stabilize-training devlog](../devlog/2026-11-25-stabilize-training.md), [ADR 0005](../adr/0005-stabilize-rl-training.md).

---

**Q1. The episode-length trap.** The v2 training curve sits at about 105 steps, but the segment is 200 steps long. Why does that *not* mean the robot falls halfway?

> [!success]- Answer
> RSI starts each episode at a random frame, uniformly between 0 and about 190. Even with zero falls, the average remaining length is about half the segment, around 100 steps. So the right measure is the fall rate, or an evaluation from fixed start frames: 20 starts, 0 falls.

**Q2. Stable but not better.** v2 never collapsed, but its tracking error (6.6 cm) is about the same as last night's best checkpoint (6.4 cm). What did the fixes buy us, then?

> [!success]- Answer
> Reliability. The good policy no longer gets destroyed by later updates, so long runs can be left unattended and the best model is saved automatically. Accuracy is a separate problem, to be tackled with metrics, helper-force annealing and more data.

**Q3. Adaptive LR.** The learning rate ended near its floor (1.3e-5). Which signal drove it down, and why is that the "safe" direction?

> [!success]- Answer
> The approximate KL stayed slightly above the desired 0.01, and the rule only raises the rate when KL < 0.005. A small rate makes each update change the policy less, which avoids the collapse. The cost is that learning gets slower.

**Q4. The helper force.** The best model uses on average 39 N of torso force (about 10 % of body weight). Why is that a problem for the paper's goal, and what could reduce it?

> [!success]- Answer
> The force doesn't exist on a real robot. Motion that relies on it isn't physically feasible, which is exactly what physics-aware retargeting is supposed to guarantee. Options: anneal its maximum (400 N down to 0) during training, or raise its penalty. ReActor's bilevel step also changes the reference so that less help is needed.
