**Q1. Data flow.** Name the path from an AMASS `.npz` file to the RL policy, block by block (B1…B5). What data (shape) passes over each arrow?

> [!success]- Answer
> B1 extract clip `.npz` → B2 SMPL forward kinematics gives **joint positions T×22×3** → B3 IK gives **robot qpos T×28** (7 root + 21 hinges) → B4 tracking env uses it as the reference → B5 PPO exchanges **obs 228 / action 27 / reward** with B4.

**Q2. Why two stages?** The IK result already looks like the dance. Why does ReActor (and our pipeline) still need RL tracking on top?

> [!success]- Answer
> IK is purely kinematic: each frame is matched on its own, with no gravity, contacts or torque limits. So feet can slide, float or penetrate the floor, and the motion may be impossible to execute. RL tracking runs the motion **in physics**, so whatever the policy produces is physically feasible by construction. Fidelity is traded for feasibility.

**Q3. Bone directions vs lengths.** In `make_targets`, each bone takes the human's *direction* but the robot's *length*. What would go wrong if we used the human joint positions directly as IK targets?

> [!success]- Answer
> The human and the robot have different proportions. Raw positions would be unreachable: the IK would stretch limbs, bend knees to compensate, or drag feet. Keeping directions preserves the *pose*; using robot lengths makes the targets *reachable*. The root is scaled by the leg ratio (0.91) for the same reason, so stride length matches leg length.

**Q4. ADR 0003 (torso bug).** The first IK version mapped the robot torso to the SMPL neck and hit 9–11 cm errors with the abdomen stuck at −75°. Why did choosing the shoulder midpoint fix it?

> [!success]- Answer
> The robot torso is one rigid block, but the human spine bends at several joints. The SMPL neck sits high, at the top of a bent spine, so matching it forced the robot to tilt its whole torso and push the abdomen joint to its limit, a local minimum. The shoulder midpoint lies where the robot's rigid torso actually ends, so the correspondence is geometrically consistent.

**Q5. Why the remaining 15–20 cm errors on dance?** Walking stays around 3 cm max, but the dance clips reach 15–20 cm. What causes it, and can better IK fix it?

> [!success]- Answer
> Joint limits: the humanoid's hip extends only +20° backwards, and the dancer's arabesques go further. No IK can fix it, because the pose is outside the robot's joint space. A different robot, or the paper's learned retargeting (which can trade a small pose error for feasibility), are the ways forward.

**Q6. Action design.** The policy outputs 21 + 6 numbers. What are they, and why is a 6-D "root wrench" there at all? Which paper idea is it?

> [!success]- Answer
> 21 = PD joint setpoints (residual around the reference angle, ×0.5). 6 = a force and torque applied directly to the torso, the **residual force control** idea (Yuan & Kitani, used in ReActor Eq. 18–19). It is a "helping hand" that keeps the robot balanced early in training or on infeasible references. It is penalized (L1) and has a deadband, so the policy learns to use it as little as possible.

**Q7. Reward and termination.** Why does the reward include a large constant survival term (+20), and why did we set `log_std_init = −1`?

> [!success]- Answer
> Without the survival bonus, early returns are negative (tracking errors plus penalties), so *falling early* maximizes return and the policy learns to fall on purpose. +20 per step makes staying alive worth it. Large initial exploration noise also produces big random torques and wrenches, which the penalties punish, so the initial noise was lowered (std ≈ 0.37) to keep early returns positive.

**Q8. Deviations from the paper (ADR 0004).** Name the biggest thing our pipeline does **not** do yet compared with ReActor, and why it matters.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Bilevel optimization:** ReActor learns the retargeting parameters (upper level) jointly with the tracking policy (lower level), using the policy's tracking result as the gradient signal (Sec. 3–5). Our reference is fixed IK. That is the paper's main contribution: it lets the retargeting adapt so the motion becomes trackable, for example by fixing foot sliding at the source instead of leaving it to the policy. Smaller deviations: residual actions, RSI instead of a learned init, a fixed learning rate, small rollouts.
